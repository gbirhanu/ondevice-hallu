# DES and triple DES

- Document: c08 (course)
- Source: Written for this project from the topics of the CoSc4035 Computer Security course slides (Jimma University), Chapter 3, part 1
- Licence: CC BY 4.0

The Data Encryption Standard (DES) was adopted in 1977 by the US National Bureau of Standards, now called NIST, and became the most widely used block cipher in the world. DES encrypts data in blocks of 64 bits using a 56-bit key. The key is usually stored as 64 bits, but 8 of those bits are used only for parity checks.

Encryption has three stages: an initial permutation of the 64 input bits, 16 rounds of key-dependent processing, and a final permutation that is the inverse of the initial one. In each round the block is split into a left half and a right half of 32 bits each. The right half is expanded to 48 bits, combined with a 48-bit round key using XOR, passed through eight S-boxes that each turn 6 bits into 4 bits, and then rearranged by a P-box. The result is XORed with the left half to form the new right half, and the old right half becomes the new left half. Decryption uses the same steps with the round keys in reverse order.

A 56-bit key gives 2^56, or about 7.2 x 10^16, possible keys, and this turned out to be too few. In 1977 Diffie and Hellman described a machine able to recover a DES key within one day, and in 1993 Wiener proposed a key-search machine costing about one million US dollars that would find a key within seven hours. The final proof came in July 1998, when the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) built a special "DES cracker" machine for less than 250,000 US dollars and broke a DES encryption in less than three days.

Because so much software and hardware already used DES, triple DES (3DES) was adopted as a stronger alternative. It applies DES three times. With two keys it uses an encrypt-decrypt-encrypt sequence, C = E(K1, D(K2, E(K1, P))); if K1 and K2 are the same, this reduces to single DES, which keeps it compatible with older systems. Three-key 3DES uses 168 bits of key. Its main drawback is speed: it is about three times slower than single DES. Triple DES has been used in Internet applications such as PGP and S/MIME.
