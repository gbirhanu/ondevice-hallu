# Authentication and AAA servers

- Document: c14 (course)
- Source: Written for this project from the topics of the CoSc4035 Computer Security course slides (Jimma University), Chapters 6 and 7
- Licence: CC BY 4.0

Authentication is the way a user proves their identity. It can be based on three kinds of factor:
- something you know, such as a password, which can be guessed, shared or stolen;
- something you have, such as a key, a card or a hardware token, which can be stolen;
- something you are, such as a fingerprint, which is costly and can sometimes be copied.
Strong authentication is important, and combining factors gives more protection.

A user name with a password is the most common authentication method, but it is weak unless strong password protections are enforced. A passphrase is a longer sequence of characters used in place of a password; because it is longer and more complex, it can be more secure. Memory cards can store information but cannot process it, while smart cards can both store and process it.

Biometrics identify a person by a unique physical attribute or behaviour. Common biometric systems include fingerprint, palm scan, hand geometry, iris scan, signature dynamics, voice print, facial scan and hand topography. Biometrics are the most expensive way to prove identity, and users do not always accept them easily.

In networks, these functions are often handled by an AAA server, which decides which users may use which services and keeps track of the resources they use. AAA stands for:
- Authentication: checking the user's identity from credentials such as passwords, one-time tokens or digital certificates.
- Authorization: allowing access to specific services according to the user's rights and local policies, for example IP address filtering or assignment, route assignment, encryption, quality of service or bandwidth control.
- Accounting: recording how the user consumes network resources, such as the user ID, the service used, the data volume and the session duration.
Two protocols are used by AAA servers: RADIUS and Diameter.
